# Settings

Spec 0012, milestone 1 (objects and attributes, #13), spec 0008 (background jobs, #8) and spec 0026 (the settings area). Every settings page sits under the settings layout route (`routes/w.$slug.settings.tsx`), which swaps the app's sidebar for the settings sidebar (the library Sidebar's settings variant): "Settings" with a back link to the page Settings was opened from (`back.ts`: main.tsx remembers each workspace page reached outside Settings in this tab's sessionStorage; with none, the workspace's home), then Account (Profile, Preferences, Sign in and devices, Notifications), Scheduling (Calendars) and Workspace (Objects, Background jobs). Every entry opens a page in Settings (20 October 2026): the Scheduling group's links into Captures (Booking pages, Forms) went, since neither has workspace wide settings yet; one booking page's or one form's settings live in its own editor, and Scheduling comes back as a group when workspace wide scheduling settings (spam protection, defaults) arrive. The old Settings addresses of the booking pages and forms (`/settings/booking-pages`, `/settings/forms`, and one page or form under them) redirect to Captures, on the same tab. A group with no page the member may open is left out (`visibleSettingsNav`); today every member may open every page. The frame stays mounted between settings pages, so the list keeps its place, and focus stays in it after a route change (`focus.ts`); the arrow keys move inside it and Tab passes it. Each page draws through `SettingsPage`: a `TopBar` with the trail ("Settings › Account › Preferences", `nav.ts` names the group by the address; the bar holds the page's h1) in the bar slot the settings frame keeps above the view (`topBarSlot.ts`, as every page's TopBar sits, with its hairline running on from the settings sidebar's head), with an object's tile and badge and the page's one primary action at its end (the owner, 21 October 2026: no title block under the bar repeats the trail), then `SettingsLayout` (`header="bar"`) with the body alone, its sections' headings h2 under the bar's h1. Disconnect on Calendars asks first: "Disconnect <account>?", what happens to its meetings and busy times and that it can be connected again, Cancel, then Disconnect, busy while the server works, a refusal shown inside the confirm. Settings opens on Account › Preferences, from the sidebar's foot (Settings, ⌘,), the workspace menu, or ⌘, (Ctrl+, off a Mac) from anywhere but a text field, an editor or a dialog. The routes load lazily, outside the first load. Each page keeps its own copy: `strings.ts` for the frame and the objects pages, `jobs-strings.ts`, `calendar-strings.ts` and `preferences-strings.ts`.

## Settings › Account › Preferences (`/w/$slug/settings/account/preferences`, PreferencesScreen)

- **Purpose**: how the app looks and reads for this person (spec 0026, AC-684).
- **Main task**: pick the theme (Appearance: Automatic, Light or Dark, kept by the theme controller, spec 0003), then the member's own time zone (searchable, "This browser’s" first), date format (Automatic, 8 Oct 2026, Oct 8, 2026, 2026-10-08, each shown as today reads), week start (Automatic, Monday, Sunday, Saturday) and start page (Home, Tasks, or any live object), and the Setup checklist on Home. Each applies the moment it changes, with no Save; the member's choices, the checklist's switch among them (`editOf`'s `homeSetup`, SA16), go through `data.preferences.change` (shown at once, put back with a toast if refused) and a quiet "Preferences saved" toast with Undo follows once it lands. The Sidebar section has no line of its own; its row's button says "Customise" (named "Customise sidebar" for screen readers, the dialog it opens).
- **Leaves out**: the language (the browser's) and the clock's 12 or 24 hours.

The member's preferences are kept per member per workspace on the server (`member_preferences`, `preferences.get` and `preferences.update`, the caller's own only, each change audited). The workspace frame (`routes/w.$slug.tsx`) reads them before its first page and wraps every page in `PreferencesScope` (`features/workspace`), a `FormatScope` over UiProvider's browser defaults: dates, relative times, date pickers, month grids and "today" follow the time zone, date format and week start. Opening `/w/$slug` (so signing in too) lands on the start page (`landing.ts`); Home is the first live object until the Home page lands. Bookings' week starts on the week start (`first-day.ts`). The records layer's relative filters (today, this week), rules, bulk targets and exports resolve in the member's zone (`zoneOf` in `@crm/data`), and so do schedules, new schedules and new pages (`memberZone` in `features/booking/zone.ts`); Bookings' week grid still draws in the browser's zone.

Built from library parts only: SettingsLayout (through `SettingsPage`), SettingsSection ("Appearance", "Dates and time", "Start page") around a Card, SettingsRow and Select (`isLabelHidden`, its label the row's title), Skeleton while loading and EmptyState if the read failed.

## Settings › Account › Sign in and devices (`/w/$slug/settings/account/sign-in`, SecurityScreen; `/settings/account/security` redirects here)

Named for what it holds (the owner, 22 October 2026: the sidebar showed Security twice). As Notion's own account page and GitHub's "Password and authentication" and "Sessions" do, the personal page says how you sign in and where you're signed in, and Workspace › Security keeps the plain word for the rules everyone follows, as Attio, Linear and Notion name their admin page. Delete account moved to Profile, the person's own page, as Notion keeps it on the account page and Linear keeps Leave workspace on Profile.

- **Purpose**: the devices signed in to this account, and ending them (spec 0033, AC-890, AC-891, AC-896).
- **Main task**: see each device as a row (its kind's icon, browser and system, "Chrome on macOS", named from the stored user agent when the session has no label; under it the rough place when the edge knew it, never "Unknown", and "Active now" or "Active 2 days ago" with the exact time on hover) and sign one out from its row, or every other one from the Signed in devices section's action at its heading, Sign out of all other devices (a sign in older than 10 minutes is asked for inside its confirm). No section carries an InfoTip: each line says what the tip did. Each ending asks first in a danger Modal. A single sign out leaves the list at once (the data layer puts it back with a toast if refused) and says so in a toast; Sign out of all other devices waits in its confirm, and when the server wants a recent sign in (STEP_UP_REQUIRED) the confirm says so in a Callout, its button now Close. After one device's sign out focus goes to Sign out of all other devices while it is still there; otherwise to the page's title.
- **Leaves out**: two factor (milestone 2), recent activity and new device alerts (milestone 4), and signing this device out (the account menu does).

Two factor sign in sits above the devices (`TwoFactorSection.tsx`, milestone 2): the authenticator app's row (an InfoTip on why it is worth it; Set up, or Turn off, a quiet secondary button whose danger Modal carries the weight, SA22) and, while on, Backup codes (Make new codes). Set up opens on the QR code (the `QrCode` atom) with its column beside it: three numbered steps, then the app's first code in large boxes under step 3, the steps going under the code on a phone; the setup key sits under it all in a FieldGroup, a `block` Code that keeps its groups of four whole with Copy inside the box. Then it shows the 10 backup codes once as a `list` Code, two columns of whole codes, with Copy (one per line) and Download, Done only after "I've saved these" (A122, SA34), with focus on Download rather than the title bar's Close (Q103). Turn off and Make new codes open with focus in the app code's boxes, so a code typed at once is taken (Q86). Each change, and each device sign out, asks for a step up inline in its own dialog when this session isn't fresh (`StepUp.tsx`: the app's code or a backup code when two factor is on, else an emailed code; turning off and new codes need the second factor itself). While the next press only emails a code (`willSend`), the dialog's button says "Email me a code"; once the code's field shows, it says the change's own verb ("Continue", "Sign out", "Sign out of all other devices"), as Delete account does (SA20). Each change that lands (two factor on or off, new codes, a device or every other device signed out) reads Recent activity's first page again (`onChanged`, `onActivityRetry`, SA35); the rows stay, busy, until the new page arrives. A send that didn't go shows the server's own sentence in a danger Callout (the code couldn't be sent, or too many codes with the minutes to wait), never on the code field; a refused code shows on the field (didn't work, or expired: send a new code). Only the unexpected says "Something went wrong".

Recent activity (`RecentActivitySection.tsx`, AC-898) reads the first page the route's loader started with `use()`, always on every render and never on a promise of its own, under its own Suspense with three loading rows; a new first page (a retry, or a read after a change) keeps the rows shown and the card busy until it arrives (`useDeferredValue`). Its `title` and `description` default to "Recent activity" and its 90 day line, so another page can show the same list under its own heading (History's "Your sign ins"); `data`, `first` and `onRetry` are its contract. React's dev only "use() to suspend in a previous render" message (Q51, 23 October 2026) doesn't come from it: on a cold dev load the router's lazy wrapper for the `/w/$slug` frame's split pending component suspends before its chunk arrives (27 of 120 settings loads on dev, none of 120 on a production build, whose React has no such check). `RecentActivitySection.browser.test.tsx` fails on that message across a first page, a retry and a new page.

Passkeys were taken out on 22 October 2026 (the owner: "remove passkey right now not needed", then "from everywhere"): no Passkeys section, no passkey step up, and two factor is the authenticator app alone. Recent activity still names passkey events and passkey sign ins recorded before then.

Delete account lives on Profile now, as its Danger zone (`DeleteAccountSection.tsx`, AC-902 to AC-907): one row, "Delete account", saying you leave every workspace and records you made stay, credited to Deleted user, with a danger button that opens a danger Modal which first lists the workspaces you are the last owner of (each linking to its Members settings, and nothing can be deleted meanwhile); otherwise it says what happens, reminds you to export first, asks for a step up (the second factor itself when two factor is on) and your email typed in full, then deletes and goes to sign in. The confirm is `useDeleteAccount`, so a deleted workspace that was the person's last opens the same one from its page (`DeleteAccountAction`, a quiet Delete your account), since Settings can't open there.

Every section has the same anatomy (the owner, 22 October 2026, after GitHub's sessions and Notion's account page): a heading, one line, then a Card of SettingsRows, so Two factor, Signed in devices and Recent activity read alike and no bare table stretches across a wide screen. Built from library parts only: SettingsSection (Signed in devices' `actions` holding the danger Button), Card, SettingsRow (an Icon leading each device: a phone, a tablet or a screen, `deviceIcon` in `security-lines.ts`; This device as a quiet Badge), RelativeTime, Button (Show more), Modal, Callout and EmptyState (`layout="row"`, one quiet line, for no devices or no activity). The pending page draws all four sections busy, so nothing moves as they arrive. The device lines are plain functions in `security-lines.ts`; copy in `security-strings.ts`.

## Settings › Account › Scheduling access (`/w/$slug/settings/account/scheduling`, SchedulingAccessScreen)

Timefix's "Sees all calendars" switch, made personal (spec 0037, AC-1266, decision 9: any member picks their own assistant), as Calendly's "manage availability for your users" and Google Calendar's "Share with specific people" put it on the person's own page.

- **Purpose**: who can see or manage my scheduling, and whose I can reach.
- **Main task**: Add someone (the bar's primary: a person picker, active members who aren't owners or admins or already added, and See or Manage with a line on what each allows; with hours set by others, Manage says it can't change them), change a level in place (an inline Select), or Remove, asked first in a danger Modal with the consequence ("Priya will no longer be able to manage your scheduling.", AC-1445). The second section lists my role's reach (owners and admins: Everyone) and each delegation I hold, which I may give back ("Give up your access to Bea's scheduling?").
- **Leaves out**: teams (not built; the server refuses a team target), custom roles' reach, and anyone else's access (the Members page's column).

Each row says where it came from ("You added", "Priya added", "Through their role"). Writes go through the data layer's scheduling store (`data.scheduling`): shown at once in every held list, put back with a toast if refused; an `access` row naming the member reads the lists again. The words are plain functions in `scheduling-access.ts`; copy in `scheduling-access-strings.ts`; the two dialogs in `SchedulingAccessDialogs.tsx`.

## Settings, Objects (`/w/$slug/settings/objects`, ObjectsScreen)

- **Purpose**: every object, standard and custom, in the workspace's order (the sidebar's), and the archived ones.
- **Main task**: owners and admins create an object, move one (drag its handle, or Move up and Move down in its row menu; the Object header's InfoTip says the order is the sidebar's), archive one, restore one from Archived (shown only while something is archived), or open one.
- **Leaves out**: an object's own settings (its page), the schema map (#56) and usage beyond the custom object meter (#38).

## New object (NewObjectDialog)

- **Purpose**: make a custom object (Projects, Partners).
- **Main task**: name one and many (the plural follows the singular plus "s" until edited), keep or change the API name (it follows the plural through the server's own `toApiSlug` until edited), pick an icon and a colour, then Create object; the object opens on its Attributes tab.
- **Leaves out**: attributes: the object starts with its text Name and the five system attributes.

## An object's settings (`/w/$slug/settings/objects/$object/$tab`, ObjectSettingsScreen)

- **Purpose**: one object, standard or custom alike, under its tile, name and Standard or Custom badge, in two tabs.
- **Main task**: add, edit, move, archive and restore its attributes (Attributes), and rename, restyle, archive or restore it (Configuration).
- **Leaves out**: changing a type (#14), and changing the API name (never).
- **Layout**: the Configuration tab follows the settings page pattern (SettingsLayout's README): a Names and look section whose Form sits two fields to a row, saved from the library SaveBar at the view's foot (the object's page holds the edits, `useObjectConfiguration`, so the bar shows on either tab and a tab change keeps them; Discard puts back what the server holds), a Danger zone section (Restore once archived) with the archive Card, then a quiet closed Developer fold with the read only API name and object ID (30 October 2026). The Attributes tab's tables run the full width.

## An object's attributes (the Attributes tab, AttributesTab)

- **Purpose**: the object's attributes as every table of it shows them: its own by position, the system ones, the archived ones.
- **Main task**: owners and admins add one (New attribute, in the page's header), open one to edit it (a row, or Edit in its menu; the address gains `?attribute=<id>`, so a link opens the same dialog), move one (drag its handle, or Move up and Move down), archive one (a confirm; not the name) and restore one from Archived.
- **Leaves out**: groups (#18) and the schema map (#56).

A meter "Attributes, N of 250" for owners and admins (a skeleton until `usage.get` answers), then the own attributes' Table (type icon and title, the type, badges for Primary, Required, Unique and Default, which reads "Default archived" while a select's or status's default names an archived option (milestone 3, AC-236), and a row menu), the system ones in a sunken Card, read only (a row opens them read only), and an Archived Disclosure, shown only while an attribute is archived: Restore on each, or, for a relationship end whose other object is archived, "Links to <Plural>, which is archived" and no Restore of its own. A move says the new place in a toast. `?attribute=` naming nothing this person can open here is removed, and nothing opens.

## The attribute dialog (AttributeDialog)

- **Purpose**: create or edit one attribute, from settings, the table's "+" header, its column menu or Add attribute at the foot of View settings.
- **Main task**: name it, pick one of the fifteen types, set the type's settings (a Number's display, a Currency's code) and options (a Select's or Multi select's, a Status's stages, starting from Not started, In progress and Done), Required, Unique and Allow multiple where the type takes them and a default, then Create attribute or Save.
- **Leaves out**: changing a type (#14). Relationship, last in the type picker for owners and admins, hands over to the relationship dialog below with the name typed so far, and a side of a two way relationship opens that dialog in edit mode.

The library's AttributeSettings, connected: one id minted per opening (a retry sends the same one), the write through the data layer and server confirmed, refusals on the setting they name (a taken API name on API name), an edit sending only what changed. A new attribute's options go with its create. An existing attribute's options save as they change (milestone 3, the editor's hint says so): each add, rename, recolour, outcome or target, move, archive and restore is one server confirmed write through `data.options`, its row spinning while it is out and showing its refusal after (a refused add's reason shows by Add option); the page reloads after each, and the dialog reads the attribute from the live list, so the rows are the server's. "N of 500" comes from `usage.get`. A system attribute, or anyone without `schema.manage`, sees it read only with Close.

## The relationship dialog (RelationshipDialog)

- **Purpose**: create a relationship from an object's Add attribute, or rename the two sides of one from its settings or its column menu (spec 0014).
- **Main task**: choose the related object and how records link, name each side, then Create relationship (or Save the names).
- **Leaves out**: changing the objects or the cardinality (#14), one way references (engine only), linking records (milestone 2).

The library's RelationshipSettings, connected. Creating (`useRelationshipCreate`): its step shows in the attribute dialog's own Modal once Relationship is chosen, so the scrim stays and Esc puts focus back on Add attribute; this object is the defining side, named with what was typed in the attribute dialog; the relationship's id is minted once per opening and sent again on every retry (a lost answer's retry gets the relationship already made); the write waits for the server and both objects' lists are read again, so the column appears here at once and the other object's table shows its side as its definitions event arrives. Editing (`RelationshipDialog`): both names come from the live list (each side carries its pair's name, `relationship.pairedTitle`, so a rename elsewhere shows), the side that was opened takes focus, and only the names that changed (trimmed) go out, in one `relationships.update`. Refusals land on the side's name (`from.title`, `to.title`), else above the settings. Anyone without `schema.manage` sees it read only with Close.

## Attribute archive confirm (ArchiveAttributeDialog)

- **Purpose**: make archiving an attribute deliberate, standard objects' included.
- **Main task**: read that its values are kept (and, for a relationship end, that the other end goes with it), then Archive <title> or Cancel.
- **Leaves out**: deleting values; Restore in the Archived section brings it back at its old place.

## Archive confirm (ArchiveObjectDialog)

- **Purpose**: make archiving an object a deliberate step, standard objects included.
- **Main task**: read how many records it holds and that they stay, then Archive <plural> or Cancel.
- **Leaves out**: deleting anything; it is restored from Archived, the Configuration tab or the object's own address.

## Settings, Background jobs (`/w/$slug/settings/jobs`, JobsScreen)

- **Main task**: see how a job is doing, confirm a counted change waiting for it, or cancel a job; each asks first. Owners and admins (`jobs.manage`) see every job, system ones included; members see their own.
- **Leaves out**: retry controls (spec 0008 milestone 2), the daily cleanup's details (milestone 3). It sits in `SettingsPage` beside Objects, its table and states inside SettingsLayout's body.

Members see their own jobs without the Started by column, which would always name them, and an "Only yours" badge beside the title says so (the page lost its description to the bar on 21 October 2026); the empty state says what shows here, for everyone or for the person's own. "Run a test job" shows for `jobs.manage` locally and in previews (AC-123): 20,000 synthetic items, every 50th refused, so the bar and the refusal summary both show. A result never shows a raw code (SW33): known refusal codes read as words ("50 you can’t change"), any other as "100 couldn’t be changed".

## Settings › Workspace › History (`/w/$slug/settings/history`, HistoryScreen, WorkspaceChangesTab, SignInsTab)

- **Main task**: find who changed what in this workspace, and when, newest first; or check this account's recent sign ins.
- **Leaves out**: Background jobs and Trash (their own pages, beside it in the nav), filters, a row's details panel and ⌘K's synonyms (milestone 2), a member's own display preferences (`member.preferences_changed`, owner decision 7), and IP addresses or request ids anywhere.

A record a booking was linked to is never named ("linked Intro call on 24 Oct, 3:30 pm to a record"). Show more reads the next 50; it is one button throughout (Show more, then Try again after a failed page), so focus stays on it, and when the last page arrives and it goes, focus moves to the table. The log is `data.audit.openLog`, opened by the route's loader and read afresh on each visit, never live; the tab retains it while shown, and while it is held the loader gets the same log back, so the router reloading the page (a live attribute change) keeps the rows and Show more's place. A row the page has no sentence for yet (a newer server during a deploy) reads "<actor> made a change" rather than failing. **Your sign ins** is Recent activity as Account › Sign in and devices draws it, through the same call (two doors to one list), under the tab's own heading, "Your sign ins", and line, "In every workspace, for the last 90 days." (RecentActivitySection's `title` and `description`, from `history-strings.ts`); its loading view (`SignInsPending`) is `RecentActivityPending` with the same heading and line over the section's own three loading rows, so nothing moves or renames when the list arrives. While a route loads, the pending page draws the tab bar exactly when the loaded one will (the workspace frame has read the access by then), with its tabs disabled until the page is there.

## Settings › Scheduling › Calendars (`/w/$slug/settings/scheduling/calendars`, CalendarScreen)

- **Purpose**: the member's own calendar accounts (Google, Outlook where it is on) and their calendars in use, so their meetings reach the people and companies they meet, their busy times keep booking pages free, and their bookings land where they choose (specs 0024 and 0026; the A59 screen, 22 October 2026).
- **Main task**: connect an account (Connect Google calendar, Connect Outlook calendar, or one Connect calendar menu of both; the browser goes to the API's start address, on to the provider's consent and back with `?calendar=` saying how it went, shown once in a Callout); pick where new bookings go under **Add bookings to** (one Select across every account, each calendar in its colour, with an InfoTip "Where bookings go"); then, per account, read its row (the provider's mark, the address, its state as a Badge with a dot: Connected, Syncing, Needs reconnecting, Access removed or Stopped syncing, and when it last synced), Reconnect on the row once it stopped, choose what teammates see of its meetings (Full details or Busy only, with an InfoTip), and Disconnect from its "···" menu, which asks first. Under the row, **one compact table per account** lists its calendars in use: the colour dot (a HuePicker) and name, with a "Logs to CRM" badge when its meetings go to the records, then the Check for conflicts and Show on my week checkboxes, and a "···" menu shown on hover with Log meetings to CRM. Each change saves at once with a quiet toast and Undo (one at a time). On a phone the table keeps each column at its own width and scrolls sideways in its own frame (Q54), so no name runs under its badge and no header sits over the next; the arrow keys reach every checkbox and scroll it into view.
- **Leaves out**: other members' calendars, and calendars not in use: an account lists only its main calendar, any with Check for conflicts, Show on my week or Log meetings on, and where bookings go (the owner, 23 October 2026, A129: the others "don't show this anywhere").

Its old addresses, `/w/$slug/settings/calendar` (where the API's Google callback still sends the browser) and `/w/$slug/settings/account/calendars`, redirect here with their search (`?calendar=`) kept (AC-686).

Laid out in the settings page pattern: the result Callout and the "Where bookings go changed" note span the page, then a Connected calendars section (the limit Callout when the account limit is reached, "You’ve connected 3 accounts, the most you can.", the Add bookings to card, then a Card per account with its calendars in a SettingsRowGroup), then, for owners and admins while calendar sync is on, a "For everyone in the workspace" section with Add people from meetings ("Applies to every member’s synced meetings.", with an InfoTip and an example). With sync off the page says "Calendar sync is off" and that booking pages still use your hours; with nothing connected, why to connect, with Connect in the empty state (and not in the bar).

Each account lists only its calendars in use (the owner, 23 October 2026: "don't show this anywhere", of the folded "Other calendars in this account" that went): its main calendar, any with Check for conflicts, Show on my week or Log meetings on, and where bookings go (`isCalendarInUse` in `../calendars/in-use.ts`, the one rule every calendar list and picker in the app follows). Every other calendar the account lists never shows: the subscribed, shared and read only ones, and also the member's own extra calendars, since only the main calendar's switches start on, so none of those can be switched on from the app. Each keeps what is stored for it; one still switched on counts as in use, so it shows. Which rows show is read once per visit: a calendar switched off keeps its row (never moving under the pointer), its toast says it won't be listed once you leave (with Undo), and it goes on the next visit; the same when the default moves off a calendar whose only use was taking bookings. An account listed with calendars but none in use (no main calendar among them) says so. Add bookings to offers only calendars in use that take bookings.

Built from library parts only: SettingsLayout (through `SettingsPage`), SettingsSection, Card, SettingsRow and SettingsRowGroup, ProviderMark, Badge, RelativeTime, Select, InfoTip, Table (`isScrollable`) with HuePicker, TruncatedText, Badge and Checkbox in its cells, Menu, Callout, Switch, Modal (the disconnect confirm) and EmptyState (no account yet, sync off, and error with Try again). Changes show at once through `data.calendar` and come back with a toast when refused; a connection still syncing is read again until it is done.

## Settings › Workspace › General (`/w/$slug/settings/general`, GeneralScreen)

- **Purpose**: the workspace's name, web address, time zone, and the currency and country new money and phone fields start in, and for owners its deletion (spec 0031, AC-822, AC-823).
- **Main task**: rename it, change its web address, or pick its time zone, currency or country; owners delete it from the Danger zone.
- **Leaves out**: a Save bar and a leave check (each change saves on its own), the logo (no row until upload arrives with #32), and members (their own page).

Everything saves at once (the owner's save rule, 21 October 2026; SS-G1 to SS-G3): the page has no SaveBar, no Discard confirm and no browser `confirm()` on leaving. One Details section holds a Card of three rows:

- **Workspace name**, a Field that saves when it is left or on Enter, once (also while that save is on its way), if the trimmed name changed (`general-form.ts`'s `nameCommit`), then reads the settings back and says "Name changed." with Undo (the same rename the other way). One saved toast shows at a time: a newer save (the name or the time zone) closes it as it starts, and so does leaving General. An Undo first reads the workspace from the server, as General isn’t live, and acts only while it still holds that change and no newer save is on its way, else it says "The name changed again since, so nothing was undone." ("The time zone …" for a time zone) (Q99). A name still typed when the page goes is saved the same way, since not every browser says the field was left as it is taken away. An empty name says "Name the workspace." on the field and sends nothing; Esc puts the saved name back; a refusal of the name stays on the field with the typed name, for Enter to try again, and a failure for any other reason is a toast too (focus may have left), with "Not saved. Press Enter to try again." on the field.
- **Web address**, read only: the whole link under the title with an icon Copy, and for `workspace.manage` a secondary Change (named "Change web address"), which opens the address dialog (`ChangeAddressDialog.tsx`). Changing it is rare and moves everyone, so it is typed only there.
- **Time zone**, a searchable Select that saves at once with "Time zone is now …" and Undo, with an InfoTip.
- **Currency** and **Country** (31 October 2026, the flow pass), the library's CurrencyPicker and CountryPicker (`list="all"`), each saving at once with "Currency is now INR." and Undo, and a Clear that goes back to Automatic (the language's region's, as before it was set; the InfoTip on Currency says so). They feed the workspace's `FormatScope` (`PreferencesScope`, read through `data.workspace.region`, after the page draws, so no first paint waits for them): a new Currency attribute and a new phone number start in them (`useRegionDefaults`), and the import reads a bare "$" as the workspace's dollar and a number with no country code in its country, when the attribute names none of its own (`TextContext.defaultCurrency`, `defaultCountry`). Stored on the workspace (`currency`, `country`, migration 0138), changed with `workspace.update` (`null` is Automatic). Not in the History list yet: a change to either writes its audit row, which History shows as "changed the workspace".

Readers without `workspace.manage` get one Callout at the top of the page, "Only owners and admins can change this.", and the fields keep their locks with no reason of their own (SS-G4). The loading state draws the same rows (and the note or Change, once access is known).

**The address dialog** (ChangeAddressDialog): the address Field after a quiet "/w/", focused on opening, starting from the address now and checked as typed through the one address check (`features/address`: a spinner while it asks, a tick and "Free", "Taken" on the field with free ones to pick under it, "3 to 40 lowercase letters and digits, with single dashes." while it breaks the rule), then the sentence on what happens to old links (they keep working and open the new address, and no other workspace can take the old one), naming the new address only once it can move there. Change address waits until a new address is free (or couldn't be checked, when the server checks it); a taken refusal shows on the field, anything else in a Callout over it. On success the page says "The workspace is now at /w/…" and opens itself at the new address; if it moved but the page couldn't open there, a warning says where it is and to reload, and nothing more can be sent. Once a new address is typed a click outside no longer closes it: only Cancel (ESC) keeps the old one. Each opening starts afresh.

**Deleting** (owners, `workspace.delete`): the Danger zone's row says "Everyone loses access. Any owner can restore it for 30 days." and its danger Delete workspace opens DeleteWorkspaceDialog, a danger Modal a click outside won't close: "Everyone loses access now and gets an email. Any owner can restore it for 30 days, then it’s gone for good.", the name typed to confirm with the quiet line "Export anything you want to keep first." under it (a link to export comes with export), then Cancel and Delete workspace, disabled until the name matches. The page then moves the owner on, with Restore in the toast; the workspace's address shows the restore page for 30 days.

Built from library parts only: SettingsLayout (through `SettingsPage`), Callout, SettingsSection, Card, SettingsRow, Field, CopyButton, Button, Select, InfoTip, Modal and Skeleton.

## Members (MembersScreen.tsx, InviteDialog.tsx, InviteLinkRows.tsx, EmailDomainRow.tsx)

- **Purpose**: Settings › Workspace › Members (spec 0031, AC-803, AC-810, AC-811, AC-818): who is in the workspace, what each can do, and who is on their way in.
- **Main task**: find someone by name or email; owners and admins change roles with the row's Select (only the roles they may give), remove people from the row menu, invite people by email, answer requests to join, resend or cancel pending invitations, and set the invite link and company email.
- **Leaves out**: leaving (the workspace menu's), teams and guests (later slices of spec 0031).

The lean pass (the owner, 21 October 2026): the TopBar holds the trail, the page's h1 and its one primary action, Invite people (`SettingsLayout header="bar"`, as the recui lane set for every settings page), and no title block or description repeats them. Tabs (`inset="none"`, so the first tab's label lines up with the table's first column) hold Members and Joining with counts (Joining's is the pending invitations plus the requests waiting); the search sits at the strip's end on the Members tab only. Joining holds, in order (SW1, SW2, 23 October 2026): Asking to join (the requests, only while some wait), Pending invitations, and Other ways to join (a Card for the invite link's rows, then one for Company email, so each way to join reads apart; set once, so last). The tab opens on Joining while a request waits, since join requests send no other notice. Approving or declining a request, or cancelling an invitation, takes its row away, so focus moves to the page's title. A lone member who may invite gets one quiet row under their own ("Just you so far. Invite your team to work together."), not a block. Empty searches and an empty Pending invitations say so in a title alone.

Built from library parts only: SettingsLayout (through `SettingsPage`), Tabs, Field (`variant="search"`, its text in `?q=` once typing pauses), Table (an Avatar and the name as the row header, a You badge on the reader's row, the role as an inline Select (the value and its chevron, no box, so a column of roles reads as text) or plain text, Joined as a RelativeTime, a row Menu with Transfer ownership and Remove from workspace, each only where the reader may; on Pending invitations a row Menu with Resend and Cancel invite), SettingsSection (Joining's three), Card, Button (Show more members), Callout (for those who can't invite, the one read only note every settings page shares: "Only owners and admins can change this."), EmptyState and Modal. Every role picker takes its options from `roles.list` (spec 0037, AC-1439), filtered by the owner rules. Someone else's role changes at once and goes back with the server's reason if refused; your own role reads as text (an owner hands on through Transfer ownership). Every other change waits for the server, then the route reads the page again. While it loads, `MembersPending` draws the same bar (Invite people disabled), the Members and Joining tabs (or the note), the search and skeleton rows.

### Scheduling access (SchedulingAccessCell.tsx)

A column for holders of `members.manage` (spec 0037, AC-1267, decision 8), after Two factor: each person's reach in a few words ("Sam and Bea", "Sam (sees everyone)", "Only their own"; `reachWords`). An owner or admin reads "Everyone" as text, since their role already reaches everyone and a control that couldn't change it would lie (timefix's rule). Anyone else's words are a quiet button opening a Popover: their role's reach, their delegations (level changed in place, Remove asked first in the shared danger Modal, the popover closing first), and Give access (Everyone or a person, See or Manage). Reads the workspace's delegations once from the scheduling store, which loads in its own chunk with the first cell (`data.scheduling.loaded()`, else `open()`).

### The invite dialog (InviteDialog.tsx)

The one invite form (owner, 22 October 2026: "use the same system to add the users across"): `useInvite.tsx` holds the rows, each row's problem and sending, and the library's `InviteRows` draws them; first run's Invite your team uses the same pair. One row per person: an email Field (text, with the email keyboard, so a pasted column keeps its line breaks), that row's role (a compact Select, Member for a new first row, the row before it's role after that) and, with more than one row, a ghost `x` that removes it (nothing is sent yet, so it asks nothing). The columns are named once above the rows, Email, and Role with the InfoTip saying what each role can do (it moved from the dialog's title bar to sit by the pickers). "+ Add another" under the rows (named "Add another person"), or Enter in the last row, adds a row and focuses it; Enter on an empty last row sends. Focus starts in the first email. A pasted list ("a@x.com, b@y.com" or one per line) fills this row and new ones, up to 20; past that, a line says only the first ones were added. Each row says what's wrong under it once left (not an address, already in the list, already a member, already invited); Invite with a bad row focuses it. The button counts the valid rows ("Invite 3 people"); one call per role. If a call fails part way, the rows already handled leave the list and a line says how many, so Invite again retries only the rest. Sent, the dialog says who was invited (announced), who already belongs, and whose email didn't go out, with Invite more and Done.

Every other Invite button opens this same dialog in place (`InviteTeamDialog.tsx`, which reads the person's role, the roles, the members and the open invitations through the data layer once it opens, loading meanwhile, with Try again if that fails): Home's Finish setting up › Invite your team, and Round robin's Invite teammates for someone who may invite (anyone else still goes to Members, which says who can).

### Company email (EmailDomainRow.tsx, Asking to join)

Other ways to join ends with Company email for holders of `workspace.manage` (spec 0031, AC-819; renamed from Email domain, SW3): one SettingsRow whose switch lets people with the reader's own verified domain ask to join ("When on, anyone with an @acme.com email can ask to join. You approve each one." while off; "People at @acme.com can ask to join. You approve each one." while on), its InfoTip saying the server's rule ("Only the domain of your own verified email can be used. Public mail like Gmail can’t."); a public or unverified address says why it can't, with the switch disabled. Turning it on waits for the server with the switch on and disabled and the line reading "Turning on for @acme.com", then a toast says it is on; turning it off asks first ("Turn off company email?"). While requests wait, Asking to join opens the Joining tab (AC-810, AC-820): Name, Email, Requested (a RelativeTime), and Approve and Decline. Approve brings them in at once as a member and reloads the page; Decline ends their request, so it asks first in the page's confirm ("Decline Grace's request?"), and they are never told.

### The invite link (InviteLinkRows.tsx)

The invite link's Card, the first in Other ways to join: the switch ("Anyone with the link can join as a member" while off; Reset link beside it while on), then while on the link to copy (Field `variant="copy"`, how many joined as its one hint), and a quiet closed Link settings fold (30 October 2026) holding Expires (never, 1, 7 or 30 days from now) and Most uses (no limit, 10, 25, 50, 100). Turning it off and Reset end the current link, so both ask first in the page's confirm; every change waits for the server, then says "Invite link saved." (`savedToast` in toast.ts). A change to Expires or Most uses adds Undo for 10 seconds, which puts the previous value back ("Change undone."). Any later change to the link (turning it off and Reset too) and leaving the tab close that toast, and an Undo acts only while the link still reads as its change left it (`linkStillReads`), so it never turns a link back on or overwrites another admin's newer value; otherwise it says "The invite link changed again since, so nothing was undone." Expires offers Undo only from Never, since an expiry counts from now and a date already set can't be put back exactly.
